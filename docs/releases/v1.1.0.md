## Plan ahead: estimated conditions up to 2030

Until now the planner could only replay days that NASA's Curiosity rover had already measured, up to sol 4995 (25 August 2026). You can now plan any day up to **two Mars years ahead: sol 6332, 29 May 2030**, including today.

For days after Curiosity's record, the planner **estimates** the conditions:

- **Weather** (temperatures, sunrise, sunset, UV) comes from the same time of year in every Mars year Curiosity has measured. Mars repeats its seasons every 668.6 sols, and Curiosity has recorded more than seven of them.
- **Air pressure** comes from the most recent Mars year, because Curiosity has been climbing Mount Sharp, where the air is thinner.
- **Background radiation** follows the Sun's roughly 11-year activity cycle, fitted to Curiosity's radiation record and joined to its latest reading. It is expected to rise from about 7 µGy/h now to about 13 µGy/h by 2030, as the Sun heads for its next quiet phase.

**Estimates are always marked as estimates.** For a future day:

- the verdict card carries an **Estimate** note;
- the day chip on the map and the *Day on Mars* section say "estimate";
- every estimated value is shown as "≈", with a likely range;
- a warning explains how the day was estimated, how well the method has done, and what cannot be predicted.

**How good are the estimates?** We ran the method as if Curiosity's record had ended one Mars year earlier, and compared its estimates for that last year with what Curiosity actually measured:

| Estimate | Typical error | Measured value inside the likely range |
|---|---|---|
| Sunrise and sunset | under a minute | — |
| Coldest air temperature | 2.3 °C | 87 % of days |
| Warmest air temperature | 3.0 °C | 87 % of days |
| Air pressure | about 1 % | 98 % of days |
| Background radiation | 0.6 µGy/h | all days |

**What cannot be predicted:** dust storms and solar storms. The solar storm test still shows whether the crew could reach shelter in time.

## Type the Mars day

*Day on Mars* now has a box to type a sol directly, beside the slider. Press Enter or click away to use it. Numbers outside the range are brought into range, with a note saying so.

## Other changes

- New presets: **Latest measured** and **Today (estimate)**.
- The radiation chart continues to 2030, with the estimated part shaded.
- Curiosity's radiation record ends at sol 4842 (March 2026), before its weather record does. Days in between now use the solar-cycle estimate for radiation, marked as an estimate, instead of silently repeating the last reading.
- Asking for a day after May 2030 gives a clear message instead of an error.

## Downloads

| System | File |
|---|---|
| Windows 10 or 11 | `MarsEVAPlanner.exe` |
| macOS on Apple silicon (M1 or later) | `Mars-EVA-Planner-mac.dmg` |
| Android 7.0 or newer | `Mars-EVA-Planner-android.apk` |

- **Android: uninstall version 1.0.0 first.** Each test build is signed with a new key, so Android will not install this version over the old one.
- The apps are not signed. Windows may warn about an unknown publisher (*More info*, then *Run anyway*). On macOS, try to open the app once, then choose *Open Anyway* in *System Settings > Privacy & Security*.
- The apps run the whole planner on your device but need an internet connection for map imagery and rover photos.

## For developers

- New `backend/app/forecast.py` makes the estimates. `backend/pipeline/check_forecast.py` tests them against the last measured Mars year and writes `backend/data/forecast_check.json`.
- `GET /api/conditions` and plans return `"estimated": true` for future days, with how each day was estimated and its likely ranges. `GET /api/site` gives the last sol that can be estimated (`sols.forecast_until`) and today's sol (`sols.today`).
- 34 backend tests (7 new), passing on both the desktop and the Android package versions.
